UNALLOCATED 0.5.0 — a first-person horror mod for **Minecraft Java Edition 26.3** (Fabric), by NULL_Threshold Interactive.

## Install
- **Minecraft 26.3** with **Fabric Loader 0.20.1 or newer**, Java 25 (the launcher provides it), at least 2 GB of RAM.
- Put **only `unallocated-0.5.0.jar`** in your `mods` folder. **The map and Fabric API are inside the JAR.**
- **TLauncher / offline accounts work.** For a TLauncher custom version the folder is `.minecraft/versions/<version name>/mods`.
- Start the game, accept the contract (type **I agree** / **De acuerdo**, or click), then press **UNALLOCATED** above Singleplayer.
- Stuck? Open the pause menu and press **I'm stuck**, or type `/unallocated next` (`/unallocated siguiente`).
- Language: Options → Language (any Spanish locale plays in Spanish; everything else in English).

## What's new since 0.4.0 (four owner playtests in one night)
- **The endings can be reached.** Dead, you find out the chat is the server's console. Every time it catches you, you
  get deeper into its process and its console answers more: `help`, `ls`, `cat` first; then `params` and its logs;
  then `ps` and `kill`. Fake server files hold what you need (its config, the studio's contract, Noor's notes, the dataset).
- **A boss fight.** The third time, the first word you type brings it down from the sky for good. It hunts you while you
  look for its process number: threads lash down where you stand, its world collapses into the void, it screams and
  closes your console. Climb to type. One `kill` on the right number stops it — for a while: the watchdog keeps waking
  it, and you must decide, in the seconds it is frozen, how it ends.
- **Three animated endings**, each followed by the whole story typed letter by letter on black: a restoration at dawn
  with music, an eternity in a white room, or being kept.
- **The monster** never chases you before the end; the first death is a scare, not a chase. It is huge now.
- **Death screen** written by it: laggy, "I agree" slips away, "Not yet" flickers in. Staying dead keeps you where you
  fell. Coming back alive puts you at spawn, with Noor's notes (and later Zuri's) to tell you what to do. Sleeping lets
  it take you again.
- **The mine** opens under you and you wake in its corrupted copy.
- **Threads rise into the sky** and sound when you pass through them; **fallen souls** replay what was recorded of them.
- **Rewritten text**: the intro (a small forgotten server still running), the diary, the signs and the endings.
- **Sound everywhere**: the ambient never dies after a death, typing makes the threads sing, the fight and every ending
  are scored; Minecraft's own music no longer plays over the horror.

Content warnings: flashing and flickering, loud sudden sounds, jump scares, psychological horror.
